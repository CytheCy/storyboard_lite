# Scene 17: Keeping Bettina from Leaving

**Location:** Family hallway outside Bettina’s room  
**Time:** Night  
**Characters:** Pavel, Mara, Bettina, Elise

---

```text
SHOT TYPE: Medium-wide, eye-level shot down a narrow subterranean family corridor, slightly off-center to capture tense body language and the doorway to Bettina’s room.

SUBJECT: Four figures in near-future underground Detroit. Bettina stands in her half-open doorway clutching a packed battery crate and a compact cooling duffel, wearing a lightweight cooling suit under a patched jacket and ultra-light AR glasses; her jaw is set to leave. In the hall, Pavel blocks gently but firmly, one hand up, the other holding a low-power lantern battery; his glasses are rugged goggle-style with HUD glow. Mara, tense but composed, steps in beside Pavel with a palm out in calm appeal, a salvaged service-robot’s tether clipped to her belt; her glasses are thin, translucent frames. Elise, smaller and anxious, stands near a wall of pipes gripping a spare battery satchel, wearing oversized round goggles, looking between Bettina and Pavel.

FOREGROUND: Scuffed concrete floor slick with condensation, painted lane stripes half-faded; a narrow cargo sled with stacked rechargeable cells and a folded enviro-hood; a tiny three-wheeled electric family cart docked to an induction port with a blinking charge indicator; a dangling tool roll with insulated pliers, conductor tape, and a multi-meter. Drips fall from wrapped steam lines into a shallow catch-pan patched with resin. A faded stencil on the wall: “Thrive Access—Authorized Only” with an arrow pointing away. A homemade door charm of printed bioplastic leaves and copper wire hangs on Bettina’s doorframe, vibrating slightly with distant air pressure pulses.

BACKGROUND: The family hallway tunnels through old Detroit foundations reinforced with new ribbed composite arches and salvaged I-beams; exposed pipes, cable trays, and fiber bundles run overhead with tag flags and QR maintenance markers. Utility lights in wire cages create pools of dim amber; a neighboring unit’s sliding door is open to reveal a tiny green niche with mushroom racks glowing under violet grow-strips, a filtration fan humming. Farther down, a narrow transport corridor bends toward a brighter, cleaner Thrive neighborhood atrium: glimpses of multilevel terraces, hanging gardens, and soft artificial daylight spill in, contrasting with the cramped, muddy passages here. On a wall-mounted notice board: battery exchange chits, a repair rota, and a scribbled warning about surface wind surges. An old municipal sign half-buried in grout reads “Gratiot Concourse—Lower.”

LIGHTING: Night-cycle underground lighting; primary is low, warm industrial bulbs and a flickering maintenance strip; secondary subtle cool spill from the distant Thrive atrium; personal AR glasses emit faint status glows; a portable lantern on the floor casts upward shadows on worried faces. Moist surfaces catch specular highlights.

ATMOSPHERE: Claustrophobic yet lived-in; audible distant ventilation thrum, occasional pressure-bell chime from a transit lift, soft market chatter echoing far off; gentle condensation mist near colder pipes; emotional tension palpable as a family intervention at the threshold of flight.

TEXTURE: Mixed grit and polish—pitted concrete, corroded metal, frayed thermal wraps, patched polymer panels, scuffed battery casings, soft fabric of cooling suits, smooth AR glass surfaces; subtle water sheen on floor and pipes; resilient green from Thrive’s distant foliage.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: no surface skyline, no sunny outdoors, no flying cars, no uniform chrome futurism, no medieval or fantasy elements, no pristine corporate sci-fi corridors, no weapons, no neon cyberpunk cityscape, no 20th-century cars, no contemporary suburban home, no empty scene, no VR headsets hiding faces, no implausible fashion, no alien architecture, no skyscraper vistas, no steam-punk gears, no clean-room sterility, no post-nuclear wasteland rubble fields.

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
