# Scene 2: News: Illiterate Youth

**Location:** News program broadcast  
**Time:** Night (news segment)  
**Characters:** Narrator/Anchor, Jennings Tonaskus, Burk Duckman, Tiz Price, P-Clone Anthropologist Lin Von

---

```text
SHOT TYPE: Medium-wide news studio shot, straight-on, framed within a polished subterranean Thrive atrium balcony overlooking multilevel terraces, with inset AR lower-thirds graphics and side panel B-roll window

SUBJECT: A composed news anchor seated behind a modular desk built from repurposed industrial aluminum and clear polymer, wearing a breathable cooling suit under a neat blazer, slim enviro-goggles styled as ultra-light AR glasses projecting subtle UI. To the anchor’s right on a vertical translucent display: Jennings Tonaskus, Burk Duckman, and Tiz Price appear as remote feeds in stacked portrait windows; to the anchor’s left, a taller holo-panel shows P-Clone Anthropologist Lin Von, a calm, androgynous figure rendered as a photoreal telepresence with micro-expressions, wearing minimalist research attire and glossy wraparound AR goggles. All participants wear distinctive eyewear: the anchor’s ultra-light frames, Jennings with chunky scavenger-style goggles, Burk in scratched industrial lenses, Tiz in sleek circular AR rims, Lin Von in matte, lab-grade visor.

FOREGROUND: The anchor’s desk integrates visible battery docks with amber charge LEDs and braided power umbilicals snaking to the floor. A compact battery crate stamped with exchange tags sits half-tucked under the desk. A palm-sized salvaged work bot perches on the desktop as a camera assistant, its single optic blinking. A folded cooling hood and a clipped-on throat mic rest beside a ruggedized touchscreen slate showing a Detroit network status bar fluctuating. A small moss-and-mushroom planter grows under a narrow LEDs strip, symbol of resource-conscious decor.

BACKGROUND: The studio is carved into a prospering Thrive neighborhood terrace: a sweeping circular atrium plunges several levels below, with broad terrace roads curving away, bordered by glass balustrades and hanging gardens heavy with ferns, trailing vines, and misting emitters. Artificial daylight panels arc high overhead but are dimmed to night segment levels, revealing maintenance catwalks, cable trays, and discreet drone rails. On adjacent terraces, tiny electric carts glide silently; a pair of autonomous work bots trundle past a battery exchange kiosk where stacked, scuffed house batteries wait under blue task lights. Farther off, a market ribbon of stalls glows warm: fresh mushrooms, hydroponic greens, pipe repair, AR lens polishing. Utility conduits, condensation-dripping pipes, and access hatches hint at the city’s layered infrastructure. A vertical elevator shaft with transparent walls shows a platform ascending, shimming slightly between guide rails. No view of sky; everything is underground.

LIGHTING: Balanced studio key light on anchor: soft, high-CRI panels with subtle cool bias; rim light separates subject from backdrop. Atrium illumination mixes dimmed artificial daylight from ceiling arrays with warm utility strips and localized task lights on terraces. Occasional flicker from power-saving cycles; remote guest windows cast faint colored spill.

ATMOSPHERE: Clean studio air meets faint atrium humidity; gentle mist halos around hanging gardens in the distance. Low ambient hum of fans, battery coolers, and distant carts implied through slight motion blur on background elements. A sense of functioning scarcity: comfort within constraints.

TEXTURE: Practical, lived-in near-future: matte polymers, brushed and scratched aluminum, rubberized cable jackets, condensation-beaded pipes, clean terrazzo-like floor panels in studio transitioning to worn concrete and patch repairs beyond. Clothing mixes cooling mesh fabrics, reinforced seams, and utility pockets; eyewear ranges from rugged goggle housings to featherweight AR frames.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: no surface skyline, no outdoor sun, no flying cars, no glossy utopian cityscapes, no uniformly ruined wasteland, no medieval or retro analog TV sets, no fantasy armor, no neon cyberpunk rain alleys, no skyscraper glass canyons, no hologram-saturated Blade Runner clones, no weapons, no crowds mobbing the anchor, no cluttered cables obscuring faces, no motion smear on the anchor, no text legibility errors on UI, no missing eyewear on any person

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
