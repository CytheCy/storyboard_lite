# Scene 9: SAB Stage 1: Lou’s Tray RB Appears

**Location:** Family dining room window overlooking terrace; terrace ring  
**Time:** Day over several weeks  
**Characters:** Gregor, Mara, Bettina, Pavel, Tray RB (robot)

---

```text
SHOT TYPE: Medium-wide interior shot from just inside a family dining room, angled toward a large reinforced window wall that overlooks a circular terrace ring deep within underground Detroit; slightly low eye level to capture table, characters, and terrace activity beyond.

SUBJECT: Gregor, Mara, Bettina, and Pavel gathered around a compact family dining table, mid-meal pause, reacting to the sudden appearance of Tray RB, a salvaged tray-carrying service robot arriving at the terrace-facing window. Everyone wears diverse AR glasses—Gregor in chunky utility goggles, Mara in slim translucent frames, Bettina in colorful ultralight rims, Pavel in matte wraparound lenses. Clothing mixes cooling-fabric house garments with practical layers: breathable cooling suits under soft home tunics, flexible knee pads, and low-profile utility shoes. Tray RB is a squat, refurbished bot with cylindrical torso, telescoping arms bearing a heat-resistant serving tray, scuffed white panels patched with mismatched plating, battery meters glowing faintly; it hovers on a low-noise omni-wheel base with grime at the seams.

FOREGROUND: The dining table with repurposed composite top, dented enamel bowls, a thermos-sized battery-powered food warmer humming, stack of exchangeable battery bricks with barcoded tags, a hand-fabricated cutlery caddy, and a tiny desk-farm tray of oyster mushrooms. A braided cable snakes to a wall socket with a rationed power badge. Drips collected in a narrow gutter beneath the inner window frame; a rag is stuffed near a seam to catch condensation from the terrace air.

BACKGROUND: Through the thick, gasketed window: the terrace ring of a prosperous Thrive neighborhood—an enormous multilevel atrium curving away, lined with broad circular terraces planted with hanging gardens, ferns, and cascading vines above sleek circular roads for tiny electric vehicles. Artificial daylight panels wash the atrium in clean brightness, while below, narrow transport corridors and older brick arches reveal patched infrastructure. People in cooling suits and AR glasses move along railings; autonomous work bots tend planters; a lift platform rises between levels. On the family’s side of the glass, architecture shows the patchwork of old Detroit—cracked concrete lintels, exposed copper and PVC runs, condensate drip lines, a faded “Battery Exchange” stencil near the door, and a ventilation grille hissing softly.

LIGHTING: Balanced mixed lighting—cool, bright artificial daylight streaming from the atrium and bouncing through the window, contrasting with the room’s warmer, dim utility strips and a single task lamp over the table. Soft reflections ripple on the glass; Tray RB’s status LEDs add small cyan highlights to faces and objects.

ATMOSPHERE: The safe, saccharine indoor air is suggested by subtle haze near vents and a faint chemical sheen on surfaces; the window seals emphasize separation from the harsh surface storms far above. The space feels lived-in but tense—family concern hangs in quiet body language as the service bot arrives.

TEXTURE: Practical, tactile materials—pitted concrete, flaking paint, rubbed stainless edges, patchwork polymer panels, scuffed robot casings, smooth bioplastic dishes, damp rubber window gaskets, braided fabric cables, lush plant leaves in the atrium, and clean composite terrace railings contrasted with the room’s worn finishes.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: skyscraper skylines, flying cars, neon cyberpunk cityscapes, uniformly ruined environments, glossy utopian labs, medieval or fantasy elements, gasoline vehicles, open blue sky, desert dunes, 20th-century CRT monitors, weaponry, excessive grunge, cluttered junkyard chaos, low-resolution textures, painterly abstraction, motion blur, overexposed highlights, character duplicates, missing AR glasses on people, anachronistic fashion like suits and ties or ball gowns.

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
