# Scene 15: Love Loop Intervention

**Location:** Hallway outside Gregor’s room; kitchen  
**Time:** Evening  
**Characters:** Mara, Pavel, Gregor (offscreen)

---

```text
SHOT TYPE:
- Medium-wide over-the-shoulder shot from slightly above Mara’s right shoulder, framing a dim Thrive–service corridor junction outside Gregor’s door, with a shallow peek through into a compact communal kitchen beyond; dynamic motion blur echoes strobe rhythms from an adjacent underground club.

SUBJECT:
- Mara in a fitted cooling suit layered with a patched denim utility vest, AR glasses with slim ultralight frames reflecting blue strobe pulses; her hand mid-reach toward Pavel, who’s half-turned back to her in the hallway. Pavel wears an older enviro-jacket with cracked thermal plating, fingerless haptics, and chunky goggle-style AR eyewear. Between them, a semi-opaque privacy door to “Gregor” flickers with low battery indicators. Gregor remains offscreen behind the door.

FOREGROUND:
- Left edge: repurposed battery crate dolly with two worn lithium blocks, tagged with handwritten exchange codes, one plugged into a wall coupler with frayed insulation and a small autonomous work bot perched on top recharging, status LEDs pulsing.
- Floor: damp steel-grate trench with condensation beading, muddy shoe prints tracking from lower tunnels; a coiled fiber-optic tether snakes toward the kitchen threshold.
- Wall: layered signage—hand-stenciled arrows to “Atrium Loop B,” faded municipal Detroit utility plates, and a taped paper note: “Quiet after cycle 22:00.” A cracked pressure gauge sweats. A makeshift door chime made from a scavenged bicycle bell wired to a microcontroller.

BACKGROUND:
- Hallway: hybrid Detroit underground—patched concrete ribs, exposed steam conduits, and coolant lines with drip catch-pans. Dim utility strips stutter with power-thrift pulsing. Far end breathes blue-white strobe spill from a crowded subterranean dance node; shadows of dancers flicker in snapshots—a fem with ribbon-wrapped arms arcing overhead, a boy laughing eyes closed, a flash of an illuminated scarf weaving past—visible only as rhythmic silhouettes through a grated blast door window.
- Gregor’s door: reinforced, paint flaked to reveal older hospital-green metal; a small screen shows “DO NOT DISTURB – CALIBRATING,” with heat haze icons.
- Kitchen beyond the threshold: tight, clean for Thrive standards yet clearly retrofit—polished concrete counters, hanging hydroponic mushrooms over a sink, a compact fab-printer humming, induction slab, and a wall rack of labeled battery canisters. A tiny electric courier trike dock with foldable baskets sits tucked under a counter. Artificial daylight panels set to warm evening hue glow over hanging garden planters. A domestic service robot arm stirs a pot while overlay prompts float in AR on Mara’s and Pavel’s lenses.

LIGHTING:
- Mixed sources: warm, steady artificial daylight from the kitchen spilling into the corridor; cold, intermittent club strobe slicing in from the far end, creating temporal freeze-frames and hard-edged shadows; jittery amber utility LEDs along pipes. Occasional spark of arc-weld repair from a side niche adds brief cyan flares. Moist air catches highlights on puddles and steel for specular pops.

ATMOSPHERE:
- Humid, faint coolant mist drifting near floor level; distant bass thump syncs with light pulses; soft chatter and market clinks echo from deeper tunnels. The corridor smells of ozone, wet concrete, and mushroom substrate. Tension hangs between Mara and Pavel as the offscreen Gregor waits beyond the door. The city feels alive—fragmented yet functioning.

TEXTURE:
- Surfaces: cracked concrete with mineral blooms, rusted pipe collars, peeling paint, greasy fingerprints on the privacy door, frayed fabric on Mara’s vest, matte ceramic of the kitchen mugs, glossy condensation beads, scuffed polymer on Pavel’s jacket, woven ribbons glimpsed in strobe silhouettes, braided cable sheathing, rubberized battery grips with embossed exchange stamps.

NEGATIVE PROMPT:
- No above-ground skylines, no flying cars, no generic neon cyberpunk streets, no clean utopian uniformity, no total ruin, no medieval or fantasy elements, no weapons, no excessive gore, no glossy chrome spaceship interiors, no VR headsets covering the whole face, no absent eyewear (everyone has glasses or goggles), no bright daylight from a sky, no 20th-century cars, no symmetrical corporate lobby, no floating hologram billboards dominating the scene.

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
