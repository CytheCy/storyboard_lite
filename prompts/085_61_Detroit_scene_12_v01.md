# Scene 12: The Love Scene on the Lake

**Location:** Virtual pleasure ship and lake environment  
**Time:** Day into evening  
**Characters:** Gregor, Mindy (female P-clone of Gregor), Quartet (virtual musicians)

---

```text
SHOT TYPE: Wide cinematic medium-long shot from slightly above eye level, centered on a sleek virtual pleasure ship drifting on an underground artificial lake inside Detroit’s Prosperous Thrive atrium caverns; framing captures deck intimacy and the surrounding multilevel terraces with hanging gardens.

SUBJECT: Gregor and Mindy (female P-clone of Gregor) seated close on a low modular lounge at the bow, jamming and flirting as day fades to evening in the simulated sky. Gregor wears a practical cooling suit under a soft recycled-fiber jacket, lightweight AR glasses, and a compact bass slung low; Mindy mirrors Gregor’s features in a feminine presentation, laughing easily, wearing an elegant cooling dress with microchannel seams, slim enviro-sandals, and ultralight AR goggles. A translucent quartet of virtual musicians—holographic cello, synth keys, brushed snare, and soft brass—floats nearby, semi-real with volumetric light grain, their interfaces tethered to the ship’s rail by glowing battery pucks.

FOREGROUND: Curved rail of the pleasure ship with embedded battery docks and braided power leads; a small crate of swappable batteries stamped with local co-op logos; salvaged robots repurposed as music stands hold tablets showing the chord progression; two tiny electric skiffs idle alongside, their fans quiet, water-beads condensing on scuffed polymer hulls. Gregor’s acoustic guitar and congas rest on shock-strapped mounts; cable clutter is neat but obviously hand-managed. Condensation pearls along the deck’s anti-slip mesh; a puddle reflects Mindy’s smile and the quartet’s ghostly notes.

BACKGROUND: The Thrive neighborhood’s enormous multilevel atrium encircles the lake: broad circular terrace roads stacked into rings, clean modern residences with panoramic glazing, and bridges wrapped in climbing vines and hanging gardens dripping with lush vegetation. Artificial daylight panels set into the vaulted ceiling transition toward evening hues, warm amber fading to cool slate-blue. Inset far beyond, narrow transportation corridors pulse with occasional tiny electric vehicles. On lower edges of the lake, older Detroit infrastructure shows through: cracked concrete esplanades, exposed pipes, retrofitted supports, and improvised docks where traders swap battery packs under dim utility lights. A small market node glows on a side tunnel—mushroom crates, fabricators humming, an autonomous work bot rolling past. No surface shots, only deep subterranean scale.

LIGHTING: Soft artificial dusk from ceiling arrays, rim-lighting Gregor and Mindy; bioluminescent strips woven into the hanging gardens cast gentle greens; low deck LEDs graze textures; reflections skim the still water; the virtual quartet emits a subdued internal glow that pulses with the tempo. Occasional warm spill from terrace apartments and garden lanterns; subtle volumetric shafts through mist near pipe vents.

ATMOSPHERE: Calm, intimate, safe-zone serenity inside a harsh-world refuge; a faint cool mist over the lake, distant drip echoes, soft fan-hum from air handlers; the sense of contrast between post-storm survival and cultivated comfort. Water-lapped acoustics cradle the jam while far-off markets murmur.

TEXTURE: Practical industrial meets cultivated luxe—powder-coated rails scuffed by use; anti-slip composite deck with condensation beads; clean terrazzo walkways on upper terraces; lush leaf surfaces with dew; aged concrete seams and bolted steel uprights near the lake edge; fabric textures of cooling garments with visible microchannels and mesh panels; translucent heels, matte instrument finishes, braided cables with patched heat-shrink.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: surface skyline, outdoor sun, flying cars, neon cyberpunk city, uniformly ruined wasteland, sterile utopia, medieval elements, gasoline engines, skyscraper horizon, desert dunes, space setting, steampunk gears, dated CRTs, low-detail faces, missing AR glasses, generic cruise liner, crowded chaos, heavy fog obscuring scene, weapons, gore, fantasy creatures, Victorian fashion, plastic toy look, low-res textures, oversaturated colors, extreme lens distortion, fish-eye, tilted horizon, cartoonish proportions, text overlays, watermarks.

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
