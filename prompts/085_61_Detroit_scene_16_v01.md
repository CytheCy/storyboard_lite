# Scene 16: Bettina Exposed to Slip

**Location:** Club porta potties and floor; Sixty-Five Tube; family home bathroom/bedroom  
**Time:** Night  
**Characters:** Bettina, Elise, Thrive (AI)

---

```text
SHOT TYPE:
- Handheld mid-wide shot, slight low angle, dynamic motion blur, framing Bettina centered-left in a crowded underground club corridor opening into a makeshift dance floor inside the Sixty-Five Tube station mezzanine

SUBJECT:
- Bettina in a sweat-damp silver dress, hair flying, wearing ultra-light AR glasses catching stray LEDs, mid-jump with two young men in patched cooling suits and mirrored goggle-style glasses; Elise pressed to Bettina’s side laughing, clutching a cracked battery pack charm; Bettina glances down at her shirt where earlier micro-dot color reflections have vanished, curiosity flickering then lost in the beat; tiny electric speaker drones and a salvaged robot DJ spinning from a crate of repurposed drive platters; subtle translucent overlay from Thrive (AI) on Bettina’s glasses HUD, ghosting a faint “Slip?” alert edge-of-frame

FOREGROUND:
- Wet, muddy concrete with puddles reflecting strobing utility lights; a line of repurposed porta potties along the wall retrofitted with condensation traps and blue biolock LEDs; cables and hoses snake between booted feet; a crate of exchange batteries stamped with Thrive-district seals; a handheld mist sprayer puffing cooling vapor; a dropped AR goggle lens half-submerged; improvised floor speakers welded into ventilation grilles; paper wrist tags with NFC squares; a narrow white beam from a jury-rigged spotlight slashing past Bettina, creating fleeting glitter across the silver fabric before failing to reappear

BACKGROUND:
- The Sixty-Five Tube’s arched, aging transit tunnel with cracked concrete ribs, exposed rusted rebar, and patched pressure doors; crowded market stalls tucked into alcoves selling enviro-suit filters, mushroom skewers, coolant gel, and boot treads; an elevator shaft cage rattling, its mesh glinting; narrow transport corridor with tiny electric pods queuing, their matte shells scuffed; a scaffolding bridge leading to a cleaner Thrive access portal above—visible through a circular opening with artificial daylight spill, hanging planters and soft-white luminaires hinting at prosperity; improvised signage: painted arrows, QR price slates, and a low-power holo placard pulsing “FLOOR A-65—DANCE”; a couple in pristine Thrive casuals—airy cooling fabrics, slimline AR glasses—watching from the terrace edge, contrasted against scavengers in taped-up suits; a maintenance bot trundling past on magnetic wheels, brushing puddles into drain grates

LIGHTING:
- Harsh, flickering utility strips; swinging warehouse lamps; intermittent needle-thin white spotlight beam; saturated club LEDs in red/blue/violet bursts; reflected shimmer off wet concrete and the silver dress; occasional warm wash spilling from the Thrive portal above, cool-white balanced against the gritty lower light; emergency amber markers blinking along tunnel seams

ATMOSPHERE:
- Dense, humid subterranean air with drifting vapor and sweat haze; bass thump vibrating cable bundles; the smell of ozone, wet cement, spilled coolant, and grilled mushrooms; joyful, chaotic energy as bodies jump in tight quarters; an undercurrent of tension as Bettina’s HUD pings a subtle warning, quickly ignored in the surge of the crowd

TEXTURE:
- Pitted concrete, peeling paint, damp and slick surfaces; frayed fabric tape and scuffed polymer armor on cooling suits; beaded condensation on porta potty doors; braided cables with patch splices; brushed steel railings marred by fingerprints; soft shimmer of Bettina’s metallic dress; gritty mud spatters on boots; smooth, sleek finish on Thrive-made battery packs and glasses creating tactile contrast with the rough salvage

NEGATIVE PROMPT:
- No surface city skylines or open-air scenes; no flying cars; no uniform high-tech gloss; no medieval or fantasy elements; no clean corporate nightclub aesthetics; no bright daylight sun shafts; no deserts or dry dust; no neon cyberpunk kanji clutter; no weapons or overt violence; no brand logos; no anachronistic fashion (no suits, tuxedos, ball gowns); no photobashing artifacts; no out-of-era vehicles; no empty, sterile spaces; no AI face distortions or extra limbs; no missing or duplicate glasses on characters

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
