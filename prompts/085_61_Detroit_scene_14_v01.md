# Scene 14: Guru Frisky on Slip Parties

**Location:** Guru Frisky show/interview segment  
**Time:** N/A  
**Characters:** Guru Frisky (host), Interviewee 1, Interviewee 2

---

```text
SHOT TYPE: Wide mid-shot, eye-level, dynamic angle from the landing platform edge as it settles, capturing Guru Frisky (host) and two interviewees framed against the underground canal-club expanse, with sweeping spotlights and strobing beams cutting across the crowd.

SUBJECT: Guru Frisky and two guests wearing diverse Detroit underground AR eyewear (one with sleek ultra-light frames, one with rugged gasketed goggles, one with wraparound translucent lenses), speaking into compact clip-on mics while half-dancing to pounding music. They’re in heat-adapted cooling suits layered over clubwear: breathable mesh panels, modular battery packs at the hips, soft armor knees, and reflective trim. All three carry small swap-batteries clipped to belts and a slim recorder node with holo-UI floating on their AR glasses. Guru Frisky gestures animatedly, hair damp with sweat, while the interviewees alternate between laughing and scanning the crowd, nodding to the beat. Their expressions are alive under shifting color—confident host energy and curious, slightly overwhelmed guests.

FOREGROUND: The cargo platform’s edge with yellow segmented light panels glowing underfoot; hydraulic brake housings, grease-slick rails, warning chevrons scuffed from heavy use. People surge past: dancers in patched enviro-suits with cooling tubes, shoulder-slung battery satchels, and club-modified salvage robots rolling low with drink racks. Close-up textures of cracked concrete, puddles that ripple with bass, scattered thermoplastic cups, and a small autonomous fan unit misting evaporative coolant. A waist-high portable security scanner pings green as bodies funnel through a temporary gate. One interviewee’s hand grips a flex-strap for balance while lights strobe, their AR UI faintly visible as vector waveforms.

BACKGROUND: The club carved into a partially excavated canal two kilometers from active drilling: a colossal trench with three-story rock and concrete walls scored by long horizontal cut lines, reinforced with riveted plates and improvised beam anchors. The canal stretches into darkness both ways beyond the club’s lighting grid. Heavy security fences and stacked crowd barriers enclose the venue on either side, topped with sensor pods and a hand-painted sign reading “SLIP PARTIES.” Catwalks and scaffolds cling to the walls, with salvage speakers bolted to pipe racks, and bundles of cable snakes bridging from a humming battery exchange kiosk. On upper terraces, a few Thrive-district onlookers in pristine cooling linens and botanical-print wraps peer down, contrasting with the dense, patched market-stall fringe at ground level selling electrolyte pouches, mushroom skewers, and DIY visor decals. Distant, deeper tunnel mouths show faint work-bot headlights drifting like fireflies.

LIGHTING: Aggressive club rig: sweeping colored beams (magenta, cyan, acid yellow) carving through aerosolized mist, plus blistering white spotlights that occasionally freeze faces in stark clarity. The landing’s floor-level yellow panels cast a warm underglow on boots and hydraulic grease. Dim utility sodium lamps and emergency strips line the canal walls, flickering subtly with load draw. Reflections ripple in shallow puddles; intermittent muzzle of battery arcing in a distant charging rack adds cool-blue highlights.

ATMOSPHERE: Hot, sweat-thick air with visible humidity haze; heat shimmer near lighting trusses. The bass pressure feels palpable—vibration in rails and cup ripples. Occasional gusts from industrial fans push fabric and hair; mist spritzes catch beams, forming brief halos. The crowd’s roar swells with each drop, mingled with the whirr of drones and the clack of turnstiles. Tension flickers as a non-dancing figure appears and vanishes in spotlight cuts across the stampede of moving bodies.

TEXTURE: Practical industrial realism—pitted concrete, chipped paint, rust blooms on brackets, muddy scuffs, frayed webbing straps, matte polymers, sweat-sheen skin, reflective safety tape, braided cable looms, soft microfiber cooling panels. Old meets new: patch-repaired walls against crisp AR glyphs and polished sensor domes. Clothing looks lived-in: stitching, vent mesh, condensation beads on goggles. Surfaces gleam where the mist settles and dull where dust cakes the beams.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: outdoor daylight, surface skyscraper skylines, flying cars, ultra-clean utopia, uniform ruin, fantasy architecture, medieval elements, neon cyberpunk rain city streets, empty club, low-tech pre-industrial tools, military battlefield, explosions, photobashed artifacts, oversoft plastic skin, doll-like faces, anachronistic gasoline cars, Victorian fashion, space suits, alien planets, bright sunbeams, sterile white lab, cartoonish proportions, text overlays, logos, watermarks, de-focused subject, bland generic nightclub without canal walls or security fences

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
