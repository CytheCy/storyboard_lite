# Scene 11: Bettina Arrives at the Underground Club

**Location:** Freight elevator to canal club; underground canal club  
**Time:** Night  
**Characters:** Bettina, Elise, Berto, Crowd

---

```text
SHOT TYPE: Wide three-quarter establishing shot from inside a rattling industrial freight elevator descending a damp shaft, doors half-open to reveal the underground canal club’s entrance beyond; low angle slightly tilted to emphasize depth and vertical layers.

SUBJECT: Bettina in a fitted cooling suit with modular fabric panels and micro-vent seams, a slim battery pack at her hip, and ultra-light AR glasses glowing faintly; she steps forward with tense excitement while Elise and Berto flank her in mismatched enviro-suits and chunky goggle-style AR eyewear; a small salvaged carry-bot lumbers behind with a printed carbon crate of batteries and coiled audio cables.

FOREGROUND: Grease-smeared freight elevator interior: corroded steel mesh gate, yellow-black hazard paint flaking on beams, exposed cable pulleys, dangling inspection light, riveted wall plates beaded with condensation; muddy boot prints on a grated floor with puddled runoff; a faded “Thrive Freight Priority” stencil and taped handbills for battery exchange rates; a coin-sized elevator cam drone perched on a magnet clip; emergency stop switch wrapped in repurposed filament tape; Bettina’s hand brushing the mesh gate; Berto’s pocket speaker rig and a salvaged mic clutched nervously; AR UI reflections ghosting in their lenses.

BACKGROUND: The underground canal club carved from old Detroit utility tunnels opens up beyond the elevator: a long, narrow waterway glistens black, edged with cracked concrete and welded catwalks; improvised railings from rebar and pipe; neon-painted arrows and stenciled glyphs direct traffic; a crowd in layered cooling gear and diverse AR glasses waits along terrace-like platforms built from pallet decking and repurposed escalator steps; tiny electric couriers hum past on a narrow circular terrace road overhead; hanging grow-baskets spill mushrooms and hardy vines down chain hoists; an autonomous bar cart-bot dispenses drinks into reusable canisters; battery swap kiosks blink with charge levels; a salvaged subwoofer wall embedded in crumbling brick; a Thrive-branded service node with a Tray RB docked nearby, its expressive polymer face idling as it catalogs crates; ductwork snakes between old steam pipes and new fiber trunks; a low arch opens to a cleaner Thrive annex with artificial daylight spilling like a soft rectangle, hinting at prosperity.

LIGHTING: High-contrast, club-night illumination: sodium utility lamps flicker in the shaft; cool cyan spill from corridor LEDs and reactive path strips; warm tungsten work lamps and string lights over the water; intermittent strobe from a DJ rig reflecting off wet concrete; subtle bioluminescent green from mushroom racks; soft, premium white from the Thrive annex, creating a stark prosperity-versus-scarcity contrast; AR glows in every set of glasses dot the crowd.

ATMOSPHERE: Humid, metallic air with drifting mist from the canal; distant thunder reverberation from surface storms filtered through shafts; muffled bass thump, clatter of bots over gratings, water lapping; smell of wet stone, ozone from charging stations, fermented drinks, and mushroom loam; a low haze of vapor catches beams and silhouettes faces; a sense of illicit celebration tucked inside industrial survival.

TEXTURE: Slick wet concrete, peeling paint, rust pitting, mineral drips calcifying on pipe joints; patched polymer fabrics, mesh overlays, and 3D-knit cooling channels on clothing; scuffed plastic casings, welded seams, braided cable sheaths; the Tray RB’s uncanny smooth face membrane with micro-creases; rubberized grips, cracked leather straps, grit-grained grating; water’s mirror ripples fractured by catwalk struts.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: daylight surface streets, blue sky, conventional skyscraper skyline, flying cars, pristine utopia, medieval fantasy elements, steampunk brass excess, uniform high-tech gloss, ruined wasteland without tech, deserts, foliage-only jungle, firearms, gore, aliens, cyberpunk neon cityscape above ground, Victorian fashion, retro diner aesthetics, 2D flat shading, low-detail faces, washed-out colors, excessive lens flares, empty environment, modern suburban homes, wide open countryside.

STYLE:
**Color palette**
- A tightly controlled teal-and-deep-green monochrome scheme dominates every frame, with near-black shadows and muted blue-gray midtones.
- Warm accents (burnt orange, mustard, cream, and the occasional red or coral) are used sparingly, so they pop: the orange protest signs, the red "BANNED" glow, the orange anatomy panel in the news graphic.
- The limited palette is what makes the set feel like one world, even across very different scenes.

**Linework and rendering**
- Confident, clean ink outlines with flat or lightly cel-shaded color fills, closer to comics than to painting.
- Subtle grain and texture overlays that give a printed, slightly aged feel, like risograph or screen-print work.
- Soft atmospheric gradients and glow effects (the hologram figure, the speed-swirl lines around the parkour scenes) layered over the flat shapes.

**Character design**
- Stylized but readable faces with simple features and expressive brows, in the vein of European *bande dessinée* or mid-century editorial illustration, rather than anime or hyper-realism.
- Recurring props like wraparound visor goggles act as a visual shorthand for the world's tech.
- Robots are sleek, matte, minimal.

**Composition**
- Dynamic, cinematic framing: dramatic leaps, crowd scenes with depth, and figures staged against detailed industrial or arena backgrounds.
- The news-broadcast scenes use a flatter, poster-like layout with panel grids, bold condensed sans-serif headlines, and cream borders, evoking WPA posters or vintage infographics.
```
